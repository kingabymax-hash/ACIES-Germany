---

## Voice Principle

This page is not written for readers in buying mode. It is written for readers in **verifying mode** — supervisors, counterparties, KYC teams, and consumers looking up dispute-resolution routes. Voice should match.

**In one line:** *The calm voice of a licensed institution stating facts about itself.*

---

## Tone

- Precise, factual, unadorned.
- Formal register in both languages — closer to German legal/corporate register than startup marketing.
- No hedging, no selling, no adjectives that reach for warmth ("passionate", "innovative", "committed to excellence"). These read as unserious on a regulatory page.
- Confident but quiet. No claims that require evidence beyond what is already disclosed.

---

## Personality

- Adult, professional, lightly traditional.
- Will not try to be friendly. Will not try to be distinctive. Will not try to have a personality.
- The personality is "this is what we are, here are the facts, here is how to verify them, here is how to reach us".

---

## Language Choices

**Use:**
- Plain statements of fact.
- Statutory citations where relevant (§34d GewO, §5 TMG, etc.) — these add credibility on a German page.
- Full company name on first mention, "Acies Europe GmbH" on subsequent mentions.
- "We" sparingly — prefer third-person / entity-level statements for the regulatory sections.

**Avoid:**
- Marketing verbs: unlock, empower, transform, elevate, deliver.
- First-person plural warmth: "we're here to help", "our family of brands", "we love".
- Filler adjectives: innovative, leading, world-class, passionate, dedicated.
- Exclamation marks. Anywhere. Ever.
- Questions as headlines ("Looking for cover in Europe?").

---

## Bilingual Considerations

- **German:** Formal (Sie, not du). Business/legal register. Use established German statutory terms verbatim (Versicherungsvermittler, Versicherungsvertragsgesetz, Handelskammer) rather than translating them casually.
- **English:** Similarly formal. British English spelling (ACIES is UK-rooted) — "organisation", "authorised", "licence" (noun) / "license" (verb).
- **Parity:** The German version is the legally-load-bearing version. English is a courtesy translation for international counterparties. Do not write English first and translate — draft in German for any section touching German statute, and ensure the English mirrors but does not invent.
